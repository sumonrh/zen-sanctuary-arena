How it works

A fair look at a small world.

Two sanctuaries, one choice. Each matchup is drawn at random. Compare them for as long as you like, then pick the one that feels more alive. “Too close” is a real answer.

Five at a time. A round is five comparisons. After each round you can choose five more or stop. Stopping reveals the current model ranking and thanks you.

Viewing. These are full-viewport WebGL scenes. Maximize your browser window — or use full-screen — before comparing. On a phone, tap A or B to switch worlds; a large screen gives the intended view.

Blind while you vote. Model names, filenames, and previous scores are hidden while you compare. The left and right sides are shuffled for every matchup. There is no leaderboard during a round.

What is recorded. Your choice, how long you looked, and a random anonymous ID stored in this browser. No account, no email, no personal details.

How the ranking works. Votes are fitted with a Bradley–Terry model — the pairwise method behind chess and preference ratings — with confidence intervals from bootstrapping. The stop screen shows a provisional top 12; the organizer page has the full table. Models are compared as families too, so a single lucky variant does not decide the result.

Tip: click inside a world to move the camera and enable its sound. If a world fails to load, use Something broke? — your report still counts.
